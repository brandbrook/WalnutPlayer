Walnut Player

Pandora, on a walnut radio for your Mac.

Walnut is a free, open-source Pandora player for macOS. A brass speaker that moves with the music, a backlit display, and real knobs, in a window shaped like a table radio.

Download for Mac Source on GitHub

Free · macOS 13 or later · Apple Silicon and Intel · Signed and notarized

Built like the radio on the kitchen shelf

Every part of the window is something you can touch. It plays your Pandora stations and stays out of the way.

A speaker that listens

The cone and dust cap move with the bass, mids and treble of whatever is playing.

Brass buttons and knobs

Stations, play and skip on latching brass buttons. LOW, MID and HIGH knobs are a real three-band EQ; double-click one to reset it.

Day and night displays

A warm cream backlight by day, glowing amber on navy glass by night, or follow your Mac's appearance.

Your stations, tuned in

Turn the tuning scale through your stations, create new ones from an artist or song, rename them, and look back through your history.

Like, dislike, tired

Rate the song from the display. Click the song, artist or album to look it up.

At home on macOS

Media keys, notifications with album art, a menu bar mode, AppleScript, Last.fm scrobbling and automatic updates.

Getting started

1. Download
Get the latest Walnut-x.y.zip from the releases page and unzip it.
2. Install
Drag Walnut into your Applications folder and open it. It's signed and notarized, so macOS opens it without warnings.
3. Sign in
Use your Pandora email and password. Walnut keeps them in your Mac's keychain.

Good to know

Do I need a Pandora account?
Yes. A free account works. Pandora is only available in the United States.
Is it free?
Yes, and open source under the MIT license.
Where did it come from?
Walnut is built on Hermes, the much-loved Pandora client for Mac, rebuilt for today's Macs with a new design.

Walnut is free and open source, based on Hermes. Built by Radu (opens in a new tab).
